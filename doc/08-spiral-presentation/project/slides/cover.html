The claim, up front. Vortex's own DataFusion benchmark (the fork's t4-read-at branch), ClickBench partitioned in 100 files, queries 20 to 34, run three ways on the same instance: Vortex reading its files from the page cache, in memory; Vortex reading them from the SSDs, its pages evicted before every run; and t4 reading them from its disk tier on the same SSDs, direct I/O, nothing kept in memory. Each bar is the sum of each query's median of the last five of twelve runs back to back, once the drives' burst after a rest is spent; the thin line runs from the summed fastest to the summed slowest of those five. Same scale for both instances. On the i7ie.12xlarge (24 cores, 4 SSDs as one RAID0, 12.7 GB/s steady) t4 from SSD takes 0.96 of Vortex's time from memory and 0.88 of Vortex's from the same SSDs; on the i7i.24xlarge (48 cores, 6 SSDs, 26.9 GB/s) 0.95 and 0.86. TPC-H SF10 on the same instances: 1.00 and 0.96 of memory. On a small i3en.2xlarge, whose two SSDs read only 1.4 GB/s steady, t4 is 3 to 5% behind memory, still 9 to 16% ahead of Vortex from SSD. All single-socket Spot instances, on their defaults, 28 Oct 2026 (dashboard: t4 Disk Tier Across Boxes).
