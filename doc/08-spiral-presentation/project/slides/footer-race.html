Not all requests are the same. Metadata reads are critical: until a file's footer arrives, no query work can start. The network doesn't care which request is critical; t4 does. Detection is simple: the last 2 MiB of the file. t4 sends that GET twice at once and takes whichever arrives first. Measured 27 Oct on ClickBench query 24, each run a fresh session reading the 100 footers, 10 runs per setting, interleaved, from S3: the slowest footer GET fell from 538 to 273 ms on Parquet and from 273 to 89 ms on Vortex, and footer GETs over 100 ms fell from 18 to 4 and from 8 to 0, of about 1,000. Your outline says the pmax fell 10x; this run shows about 2x and 3x. Use the 10x only if you have the run behind it. From GCS, read across clouds, racing also cut the slowest first byte, but the copies slowed bodies two to three times at the median. Racing four did no better than two (doc/06-read-modes.md).
