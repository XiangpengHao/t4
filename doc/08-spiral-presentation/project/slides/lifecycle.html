A block’s life

Every read is rounded out to 1 MiB blocks. A block lives in one tier at a time.

Remote

S3 · GCS

Memory

RAM

Disk

Local SSDs

Dropped

1 · fetched

2 · demoted

3 · 2nd hit

4 · evicted

The default policy is a lock-free CLOCK per tier. A fetched block goes to memory. Memory’s victims go to disk. A disk block moves up on its 2nd hit.

Eviction runs when a block comes in: at most 8 victims a batch, never more than 1/16 of a tier.

The life cycle. Memory and disk both start empty. As queries run, their byte ranges are rounded out to 1 MiB blocks and fetched from S3. Once a block is fetched, a policy decides where it goes: memory, disk, or nowhere. Once memory or disk is full, the policy picks victims: memory's go down to disk, disk's are dropped. A disk block that is hit a second time moves back up to memory. The default policy is a lock-free CLOCK per tier (src/cache/policy/clock.rs); eviction runs at insertion, at most 8 victims a batch, never more than 1/16 of the tier, up to 4 rounds (doc/03-cache.md). Placement is a trait, so a user can plug in their own: on_remote_read, on_memory_hit, on_disk_hit.
